🖥️ New Mac mini and Mac Studio — Built for On-Device AI

Apple announced the new Mac mini with the all-new M6 and powerful M5 Pro, delivering a dramatic boost in performance and even more versatility in its supersmall desktop design. With M6, Mac mini now delivers up to 4x faster AI performance, 2x faster storage and graphics, and 40 percent faster CPU performance, including up to 13.5x faster LLM prompt processing in LM Studio compared to Mac mini with M1.

Apple also introduced the new Mac Studio, featuring M5 Max and the all-new M5 Ultra, Apple's most powerful Mac ever. Mac Studio now delivers up to 4.3x faster AI performance, up to 2x faster storage, up to 1.8x faster graphics, and up to 1.3x faster CPU speed, along with higher memory bandwidth.

Mac mini starts at $799 for education with the all-new M6, or $1,599 for education with M5 Pro.
Mac Studio starts at $2,299 for education with M5 Max, or $5,099 for education with M5 Ultra. A 512GB unified memory configuration arrives in late October.

Both lines support Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6. Mac mini now includes upgraded 2.5Gb Ethernet standard, with a 10Gb option available, and both models add genlock support through USB-C for precise synchronization between a display and camera, including iPhone 17 Pro. On connectivity, Mac mini with M6 includes three Thunderbolt 4 ports, while Mac mini with M5 Pro steps up to three Thunderbolt 5 ports; Mac Studio includes Thunderbolt 5 across both M5 Max and M5 Ultra. Thunderbolt 5 also enables clustering multiple Mac Studio systems together for distributed AI inference, with Apple citing up to 3x faster performance across four clustered systems compared to a single system.

Mac Studio also steps up to a third-generation ray-tracing engine across both M5 Max and M5 Ultra, delivering faster, more realistic rendering for 3D, VFX, and design workflows.

Mac mini and Mac Studio are available for pre-order, arriving September 22.

DDM & Fleet Update Management

Content Caching Monitoring Reports Schema Now Available

At WWDC26, the What's New for IT site mentioned a new option in the Content Caching Configuration section: the ability for content caching servers to push their own status reports to an HTTPS endpoint, opening the door to custom monitoring dashboards and integration with your existing observability tools. Now the schema for those reports is available to review: Content Caching metrics_report.json →

Deferral vs. Enforcement: A Real Behavior Worth Knowing

A field-engineering thread surfaced a DDM behavior that's easy to miss: once a SoftwareUpdateEnforcementSpecific declaration lands on a device, it always overrides any MDM-configured deferral period, including tools like Intune's "Latest Version Policy." This is expected behavior, not a bug, but it catches admins off guard. Apple's own documentation covers it in detail: deploying software updates using declarative management and the recently updated install and enforce software updates guide.

Blueprints Keep Getting More Capable

Philip Ross has two new pieces worth a look if you're leaning into Jamf Blueprints: stacking DDM predicates to auto-activate declarations without endless blueprint/scoping-group combinations, and retroactively activating the macOS user channel on already-enrolled devices, so user-scope declarations can finally apply. And if you're still relying on legacy software-update MDM commands, Jon Brown makes the case for moving on: those commands stop working entirely on devices running 27.0 operating systems, and Blueprints are the replacement model.

Teachers' Essential Guide to STEM with Apple Intelligence

Common Sense Education's latest guide looks at how Apple Intelligence can support STEM instruction without getting in the way of it. Good STEM teaching is inquiry-driven: students question assumptions, test ideas, and revise their thinking as they go. The guide argues AI tools fit naturally into that process. They can help generate questions, organize information, and draft explanations, while leaving the actual reasoning and discovery to students.

For colleges of education and teacher-prep programs, this is a useful one to have on hand as faculty think through how Apple Intelligence fits into STEM methods courses and student teaching placements.

🌟 Accessibility Spotlight

Special Education Enrollment Is Rising — Here's How Higher Ed Can Get Ahead of It

Special education enrollment is climbing nationally, both in the number of students served and in how much of the school day they spend in general education classrooms. A GAO report found inclusion rates in general ed rose 25% between 2012-13 and 2023-24, and school districts nationwide are investing accordingly to keep pace.

Today's K-12 students are tomorrow's college students, and that means growing demand for accommodations and support services on campus. The good news: with the right technology in place, well-planned support and Apple's built-in accessibility features can make a real difference for these students as they transition to higher education.

A Smartphone Navigation App for People with Blindness and Low Vision

Here's how one university is leveraging technology to help blind and low-vision users navigate as pedestrians. Harvard Ph.D. student Raymond Liu, inspired by his older sibling's experience losing their sight later in life, built Mobilio: a smartphone app that uses computer vision to identify sidewalks, crosswalks, and obstacles, then guides users safely using specialized audio feedback, no extra equipment required.
